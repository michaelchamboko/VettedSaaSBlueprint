# Vetted SaaS Blueprint — Growth, Distribution and Audience Development Strategy

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Vetted SaaS Blueprint turns its daily connected three-page decision set into sustained discovery, trusted distribution, repeat visits, qualified commercial action, and long-term audience value.

It governs:

- demand sensing;
- publication-led distribution;
- organic search and Answer Engine Optimization;
- AI crawler access, monitoring, control, and future monetisation;
- internal discovery and topic relationships;
- owned-audience development;
- email and evidence-change alerts;
- earned authority, citations, and partnerships;
- community and visual distribution experiments;
- machine-readable feeds, embeds, and approved reuse;
- post-publication refresh and redistribution;
- growth measurement and experiment governance;
- ethical affiliate conversion;
- distribution resilience when a channel or dependency fails.

The strategy exists to attract real people who need help making software decisions. It is not a plan for manufacturing traffic, impressions, or content volume without user value.

## 2. Authority and boundaries

This document is authoritative for growth, distribution, audience development, and channel measurement.

It does not redefine:

- the product promise or functional requirements in the Master PRD;
- the three public page types in the Product Roadmap and Master PRD;
- evidence acquisition rules in the Research Operations document;
- editorial assembly and refresh rules in the Editorial Operations document;
- design and interaction rules in the Design System;
- deployment mechanics in the Architecture and Implementation Standards documents.

Where a distribution tactic conflicts with truthfulness, editorial independence, user usefulness, privacy, accessibility, or the daily delivery obligation, those requirements take priority.

The words **MUST**, **SHOULD**, and **MAY** are deliberate:

- **MUST** means mandatory.
- **SHOULD** means expected unless a documented reason supports a safer or more useful alternative.
- **MAY** means optional, experimental, or dependent on evidence of value.

## 3. Non-negotiable growth contract

### 3.1 Daily connected release

Every active operating day MUST publish and deploy one connected decision set:

1. one **Decision Hub**;
2. one **Solution Hub**;
3. one **Decision Scenario**.

The three pages MUST address the same customer problem, outcome, category, or buying situation. They are one distribution unit, not three unrelated articles.

### 3.2 Delivery never waits for evidence completeness

Evidence availability MUST NOT stop publication or deployment.

The set publishes with its actual evidence state:

- confirmed;
- partially confirmed;
- conflicting;
- insufficient evidence;
- not yet researched;
- stale;
- not applicable.

Evidence state changes what the pages may claim, how confidence is presented, and which follow-up work is created. It never changes the obligation to deliver.

No worker may fabricate evidence, certainty, comparisons, scores, user sentiment, or recommendations to make an incomplete state appear complete.

### 3.3 Publish, learn, update, redistribute

Every page and derived asset MUST remain versioned and revisitable.

When new, corrected, conflicting, or materially changed evidence appears, the system MUST:

1. identify the affected claims, pages, assets, and audience segments;
2. create a refresh task;
3. update the live decision set;
4. preserve the earlier state in history;
5. update affected internal links and structured outputs;
6. redistribute the material change through relevant channels;
7. continue the next daily release without waiting for the refresh programme to finish.

### 3.4 Open public value

Core decision reasoning MUST remain useful without registration, payment, an email address, or an affiliate click.

Saved decisions, topic follows, evidence-change alerts, downloadable assets, and personalised reports MAY request consent or contact details when the user explicitly asks for that additional value.

The product has no mandatory onboarding funnel. Audience activation means that a real visitor recognises a relevant situation, engages with the reasoning, reaches a clearer decision state, returns for updates, requests a useful asset, or takes a qualified next action.

## 4. Growth thesis

Vetted SaaS Blueprint grows by becoming the most useful, trustworthy, and reusable answer to real software-buying decisions.

The growth system therefore compounds five assets:

1. **Decision coverage** — more real situations answered through connected decision sets.
2. **Structured intelligence** — reusable relationships between problems, constraints, products, evidence, and scenarios.
3. **Trust** — visible methodology, provenance, uncertainty, corrections, and editorial independence.
4. **Distribution surfaces** — human, search, answer-engine, email, partner, community, and machine-readable access.
5. **Audience memory** — voluntary ways for people to save, follow, revisit, and receive material updates.

Publishing volume is an operating input. Confident evidence-supported decisions by real people are the intended outcome.

## 5. Audience and demand model

### 5.1 Primary audience states

Distribution should reach people who:

- recognise a software-related pain but have not defined the problem;
- know the outcome they need but not the solution category;
- are comparing approaches or products;
- need to justify a purchase internally;
- are deciding whether to switch, upgrade, migrate, or wait;
- need evidence that a product fits a specific constraint;
- want updated guidance after products, pricing, integrations, or evidence change.

### 5.2 Demand-sensing capability

The system MUST maintain a demand record built from lawful and appropriate signals such as:

- real customer questions;
- search queries and query gaps;
- onsite searches and zero-result searches;
- community discussions;
- support and implementation language;
- affiliate-intent patterns;
- user-submitted questions;
- internal-link journeys;
- evidence-change events;
- recurring comparison and switching questions;
- known seasonal or regulatory decision triggers.

Demand sensing identifies questions. It does not prove answers.

### 5.3 Opportunity scoring

Candidate daily sets SHOULD be scored against:

- decision pain and urgency;
- specificity of the user situation;
- usefulness of a connected three-page treatment;
- evidence state;
- opportunity to reduce uncertainty;
- distribution fit;
- update value;
- commercial relevance;
- production and maintenance effort;
- relationship to existing decision sets.

Weak or missing evidence does not remove a real question from publication. It changes the page treatment and creates follow-up research.

## 6. Publication-to-distribution loop

Every daily set MUST move through this loop:

```text
real demand
  -> selected decision
  -> connected three-page set
  -> production deployment
  -> distribution asset generation
  -> channel delivery
  -> decision and distribution telemetry
  -> audience and outcome feedback
  -> evidence and page refresh
  -> redistribution
```

The production deployment is the source release. Channel assets derive from it and MUST NOT become independent sources of truth.

## 7. Distribution asset compiler

### 7.1 One source, multiple forms

Each daily set SHOULD generate reusable assets from the same structured decision record.

Eligible assets include:

- direct-answer summaries;
- question-and-answer blocks;
- conditional recommendation cards;
- evidence and uncertainty summaries;
- decision criteria tables;
- comparison matrices;
- decision trees;
- checklists;
- scorecards;
- implementation or migration briefs;
- email modules;
- update alerts;
- social and community summaries;
- saveable visual cards;
- structured metadata;
- machine-readable feeds;
- partner-ready embeds;
- downloadable decision reports.

### 7.2 Asset integrity

Every derived asset MUST retain:

- the canonical page or decision-set identifier;
- the applicable user context;
- evidence and review dates where relevant;
- the evidence state;
- material conditions and limitations;
- affiliate disclosure where required;
- a route to the complete public reasoning;
- update compatibility.

An asset must never convert a conditional recommendation into a universal claim.

### 7.3 Page-specific asset roles

The **Decision Hub** supplies problem framing, question clusters, diagnostic summaries, and decision-factor assets.

The **Solution Hub** supplies conditional solution maps, evaluation criteria, best-fit patterns, and implementation-path assets.

The **Decision Scenario** supplies narrow comparisons, switching or upgrade guidance, budget and constraint cards, and shareable scenario answers.

## 8. Channel portfolio

### 8.1 Owned website and internal discovery

The website is the canonical distribution surface.

Every daily set MUST include:

- working navigation between its three pages;
- contextual links to related existing decisions;
- clear next-decision routes;
- stable canonical URLs;
- meaningful crawlable HTML;
- accurate sitemap inclusion;
- relevant structured metadata;
- visible publication and review dates.

Internal links SHOULD be updated automatically or through the daily workflow when a new set changes the relationship map.

### 8.2 Organic search

Search distribution MUST prioritise outcome-shaped and situation-shaped demand rather than generic high-volume product-review terms.

Pages SHOULD:

- use the language of the real decision;
- answer the primary question directly;
- expose useful supporting detail;
- maintain stable identity through updates;
- avoid duplicating the same intent across competing pages;
- preserve canonical ownership when content is syndicated;
- remain fast, accessible, and understandable without client-only interactions.

### 8.3 Answer engines and AI-mediated discovery

The system MUST optimise for accurate extraction, citation, attribution, and recommendation without writing for machines at the expense of people.

Pages SHOULD provide:

- concise direct answers near the relevant question;
- explicit entities and product names;
- structured conditions and constraints;
- clear methodology;
- visible uncertainty;
- stable claim and evidence identifiers;
- extractable summaries and tables;
- source and update provenance;
- content that remains meaningful when quoted outside the page.

The system SHOULD monitor whether answer engines:

- discover the page;
- cite it;
- attribute it correctly;
- preserve its conditions and uncertainty;
- recommend the site, a product, or another source;
- send a qualified referral;
- repeat stale or corrected information.

Being cited and being recommended are separate outcomes and must be measured separately.

### 8.4 Email and evidence-change alerts

Email distribution MUST be tied to requested value.

Eligible reasons to request an email address include:

- delivering a personalised decision report;
- saving a shortlist or decision asset;
- following a topic or product;
- receiving a material evidence-change alert;
- receiving an implementation or migration checklist;
- receiving an update to a previously viewed decision.

Email MUST NOT block access to the core public reasoning.

### 8.5 Visual and saveable distribution

Pinterest and similar visual channels MAY be tested when the decision naturally produces saveable assets such as:

- decision trees;
- checklists;
- comparison frameworks;
- workflow diagrams;
- budget planners;
- implementation roadmaps;
- conditional choice cards.

A visual-channel experiment continues only when it produces qualified visits, saves, return behaviour, asset requests, or commercial actions. Impressions alone are insufficient.

### 8.6 Communities

Community distribution MUST be contextual, useful, transparent, and non-spammy.

Workers MAY:

- answer a real question with relevant reasoning;
- share a methodology or decision framework;
- request correction or missing context;
- contribute original data or a useful tool;
- invite voluntary feedback on a public decision asset.

Workers MUST NOT impersonate users, conceal affiliation, manufacture consensus, mass-post links, or treat community statements as proof of a product claim.

### 8.7 Earned authority and partnerships

The system SHOULD earn third-party authority through:

- original methods and datasets;
- transparent evaluation frameworks;
- expert review and contribution;
- credible citations;
- useful partner resources;
- co-created decision assets;
- public corrections and update history;
- embeddable tools or evidence cards;
- consistent entity and methodology descriptions across trusted surfaces.

Partners, vendors, or affiliate programmes may distribute the work but may not purchase a score, ranking, conclusion, or undisclosed editorial change.

### 8.8 Feeds, embeds, and approved reuse

The system SHOULD remain ready to expose controlled machine-readable distribution through:

- RSS or equivalent update feeds;
- structured decision feeds;
- approved partner APIs;
- embeddable decision cards;
- evidence-change feeds;
- licensed content packages;
- agent-readable summaries.

Approved reuse MUST preserve attribution, canonical ownership, evidence state, applicable conditions, and update behaviour.

### 8.9 Paid distribution

Paid distribution MAY be used for controlled learning where organic or owned signals are too slow to answer a specific question.

Every paid test MUST define:

- the audience and decision intent;
- the asset and landing decision set;
- the hypothesis;
- the primary metric;
- the budget boundary;
- the stopping rule;
- the learning that will change future distribution.

Paid reach must not be used to manufacture apparent authority.

## 9. AI crawler and machine-access strategy

### 9.1 Three distinct objectives

Crawler strategy MUST distinguish:

1. **Discovery** — legitimate systems can find and understand the public work.
2. **Citation and referral** — systems attribute, reuse, recommend, or send qualified users.
3. **Commercial access** — a crawler, agent, platform, or partner pays for approved access or use.

These outcomes are related but not interchangeable.

### 9.2 Crawler policy matrix

Crawler decisions MUST use a maintained policy record with at least:

| Field | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| Crawler or consumer | Identified machine, platform, partner, or unknown actor |
| Purpose | Search indexing, answer retrieval, training, monitoring, commercial reuse, or unknown |
| Behaviour | Pages requested, frequency, compliance, and technical impact |
| User value | Discovery, attribution, referral, or no demonstrated value |
| Commercial value | Current or potential paid relationship |
| Rights position | Allowed, restricted, licensed, blocked, or unresolved |
| Current action | Allow, monitor, rate-limit, block, or charge |
| Review date | When the policy must be reconsidered |

### 9.3 Default posture

The default is neither allow everything nor block everything.

The system SHOULD:

- allow legitimate discovery and indexing that supports human access;
- allow or monitor answer-engine access that produces attribution, citation, referral, or strategic learning;
- rate-limit abusive or operationally harmful access;
- block unlawful, deceptive, non-compliant, or persistently abusive access;
- preserve evidence of crawler behaviour and policy changes;
- review access by purpose rather than company name alone.

### 9.4 Charging and licensing readiness

The platform MUST preserve the ability to introduce:

- Pay Per Crawl;
- Pay Per Use or pay-per-query access;
- content licensing;
- approved partner feeds;
- paid agent retrieval;
- commercial access to structured decision intelligence.

Charging remains optional until the economics and user-acquisition effect are understood.

The system SHOULD consider charging when:

- the consumer receives repeatable commercial value;
- attribution or referral is absent or inadequate;
- access creates material infrastructure or rights cost;
- a reliable charging mechanism exists;
- discovery and search visibility can be preserved appropriately;
- the commercial relationship does not compromise editorial independence;
- measurement can distinguish paid access from ordinary public crawling.

Crawler monetisation MUST NOT become a dependency for daily publication.

### 9.5 Machine-access resilience

No single crawler convention, vendor, protocol, or payment platform may become the only discovery path.

The system SHOULD preserve:

- crawlable public HTML;
- sitemaps;
- controlled robots directives;
- stable canonical URLs;
- structured metadata;
- feeds or APIs where approved;
- direct human navigation;
- versioned crawler policy history.

## 10. Distribution orchestration

### 10.1 Daily release sequence

For each daily set, the distribution workflow MUST:

1. deploy the canonical three pages;
2. verify public availability and navigation;
3. register or update sitemap and internal relationships;
4. generate eligible derived assets;
5. select channels based on decision and format fit;
6. publish or queue channel outputs;
7. record destinations and asset versions;
8. start measurement;
9. record failures without cancelling the canonical release;
10. schedule later distribution and refresh actions.

### 10.2 Channel selection

Every channel decision SHOULD consider:

- where the relevant audience already expresses the problem;
- whether the format fits channel behaviour;
- whether participation is legitimate and sustainable;
- whether the channel preserves conditions and evidence state;
- whether useful measurement exists;
- the expected maintenance burden;
- privacy, licensing, and disclosure requirements.

The system must not publish everywhere by default.

### 10.3 Distribution fallback

A failed optional channel, integration, crawler, feed, email provider, or analytics service MUST NOT block the daily production deployment.

The system must:

1. preserve the canonical release;
2. record the failed distribution action;
3. use a simpler lawful fallback where useful;
4. queue a retry or repair;
5. continue other eligible distribution actions.

## 11. Trust distribution

Trust signals MUST travel with the work rather than remaining isolated on a methodology page.

Relevant pages and assets SHOULD expose:

- responsible owner or author;
- methodology version;
- publication and review dates;
- evidence state;
- important limitations;
- affiliate or commercial conflicts;
- material corrections;
- links to deeper reasoning and evidence;
- AI assistance disclosure where required or useful;
- update-subscription options where appropriate.

Detection tools alone do not prove authorship or quality. Human accountability, evidence traceability, and correction behaviour remain the governing controls.

## 12. Refresh and redistribution

### 12.1 Material change triggers

Redistribution SHOULD be considered when:

- a recommendation changes;
- confidence materially increases or decreases;
- pricing, packaging, integrations, or availability change;
- a source is corrected, withdrawn, or contradicted;
- a previously missing question receives evidence;
- a legal, compliance, security, or accessibility issue changes the decision;
- a new decision scenario becomes relevant;
- an important broken link or stale claim is repaired.

### 12.2 Redistribution treatment

The system MUST update the canonical page first.

It then updates affected:

- summaries;
- decision assets;
- email or topic alerts;
- feeds;
- partner embeds;
- internal links;
- social or community references where correction is practical;
- answer-engine monitoring prompts.

Redistribution must describe the material change rather than pretending the page is entirely new.

## 13. Measurement framework

### 13.1 North-star outcome

The north-star outcome is:

> Increase the number of real people who reach a confident, evidence-supported software decision.

No single metric proves this outcome. The system uses behavioural, quality, commercial, and feedback signals together.

### 13.2 Production metrics

- connected daily sets deployed;
- pages and derived assets published;
- distribution actions completed or failed;
- time from canonical deployment to first distribution;
- refreshes and redistributions completed;
- unresolved distribution failures.

### 13.3 Discovery metrics

- indexed pages;
- query coverage;
- organic impressions and qualified visits;
- internal discovery paths;
- answer-engine visibility;
- citation presence;
- recommendation presence;
- crawler activity;
- partner or community referrals;
- direct and branded visits.

### 13.4 Decision-engagement metrics

- relevant-scenario identification;
- decision-path completion;
- evidence-detail engagement;
- comparison or criteria interaction;
- shortlist or saved-decision activity;
- helpful or not-helpful feedback;
- return visits after updates;
- low no-result abandonment.

### 13.5 Owned-audience metrics

- decision-report requests;
- topic follows;
- evidence-change alert subscriptions;
- qualified email capture;
- update open and return rates;
- saved-decision continuation;
- voluntary post-decision feedback.

### 13.6 Commercial metrics

- qualified affiliate click-through;
- affiliate conversion where available;
- revenue per qualified visit;
- assisted commercial actions;
- partner-referred actions;
- licensing or machine-access revenue;
- revenue concentration by vendor;
- recommendations unaffected by affiliate availability.

### 13.7 Quality and trust metrics

- claim traceability;
- evidence freshness;
- partial, conflicting, missing, and stale states;
- material correction rate;
- update completion time;
- user-reported confidence;
- recommendation usefulness after the decision;
- disclosure completeness.

### 13.8 Distributed and zero-click influence

The system SHOULD measure influence that occurs without a site visit through available signals such as:

- answer-engine citations;
- share of monitored answers;
- accurate entity and methodology descriptions;
- recommendation inclusion;
- branded search or direct-visit change;
- partner reuse;
- community references;
- downstream assisted action.

Zero-click influence must not be reported as proven causation without an appropriate experiment or evidence trail.

## 14. Experiment governance

Every material growth experiment MUST record:

- the problem or opportunity;
- the target audience and decision state;
- the hypothesis;
- the channel and asset;
- the baseline;
- one primary metric;
- guardrail metrics;
- budget and time boundary;
- minimum useful observation threshold;
- stopping rule;
- result;
- decision to retain, revise, or end the tactic;
- implications for future daily sets.

Experiments SHOULD test channel-product fit, not merely whether a channel can generate impressions.

Where traffic is too low for reliable statistical inference, the result must be labelled directional and supported with qualitative evidence.

## 15. Roles and accountability

### 15.1 Editorial orchestrator

- selects the daily opportunity;
- protects the connected three-page requirement;
- coordinates canonical publication and distribution;
- ensures failures create tasks rather than stopping delivery.

### 15.2 Distribution worker

- generates channel-appropriate assets from the canonical record;
- applies channel rules and disclosures;
- records destinations, versions, and outcomes;
- avoids independent unsynchronised claims.

### 15.3 Growth analyst

- maintains metrics and baselines;
- distinguishes discovery, citation, recommendation, engagement, and conversion;
- reviews experiments and anomalies;
- prevents vanity metrics from becoming decision authority.

### 15.4 Crawler and access steward

- maintains crawler identification and policy records;
- monitors access behaviour and technical impact;
- recommends allow, monitor, rate-limit, block, or charge treatment;
- preserves discovery while evaluating commercial access.

### 15.5 Refresh worker

- detects material evidence and product changes;
- updates affected canonical pages and assets;
- triggers redistribution;
- preserves version and correction history.

### 15.6 Product owner

The product owner retains authority over:

- new paid channels;
- material partnership commitments;
- crawler charging and licensing activation;
- changes to editorial-commercial separation;
- material privacy or consent changes;
- changes to the daily delivery obligation.

## 16. Ethical, privacy, and commercial boundaries

Growth and distribution MUST NOT use:

- fabricated engagement or testimonials;
- undisclosed affiliate relationships;
- manipulated rankings;
- false scarcity or urgency;
- impersonation or covert community promotion;
- purchased links presented as earned authority;
- email capture before basic public value;
- dark patterns;
- unauthorised personal data;
- uncontrolled sharing of user decision context;
- vendor payment as a reason to change evidence or fit conclusions.

Anonymous aggregate measurement SHOULD be preferred where it answers the question. Personal data requires a defined purpose, consent basis, retention rule, and deletion path.

## 17. Failure modes and required responses

| Failure | Required response |
|---|---|
| Missing evidence | Publish the actual missing-evidence state, create research and refresh tasks, continue distribution honestly |
| Conflicting evidence | Publish the conflict and conditional reasoning, monitor for resolution |
| Distribution channel failure | Preserve canonical deployment, record failure, use or queue a fallback |
| Search or AI visibility loss | Diagnose technical eligibility, content fit, authority, and external change separately |
| Incorrect external reuse | Correct the canonical source, request correction where practical, publish an update record |
| Stale asset | Update or withdraw the derived asset while preserving the canonical page and history |
| Affiliate programme loss | Retain the useful recommendation, update the commercial action, offer non-affiliate alternatives |
| Crawler abuse | Rate-limit or block the actor without indiscriminately damaging legitimate discovery |
| Analytics loss | Continue delivery, preserve available logs, restore measurement, label the gap |
| Low-performing channel | End or revise the tactic according to its experiment rule |
| Daily set at risk | Reduce complexity, publish the smallest truthful connected set, queue deeper improvements |

## 18. Minimum daily distribution record

Each daily set MUST leave a record containing:

- decision-set identifier;
- canonical URLs;
- user problem or scenario;
- evidence state;
- deployed version and time;
- derived assets generated;
- channels selected and why;
- distribution destinations;
- disclosure requirements;
- crawler and indexing treatment where relevant;
- measurement start state;
- failed actions and retry tasks;
- refresh triggers;
- owner or responsible worker.

## 19. Definition of done

This strategy is operationally satisfied for a daily set when:

1. the connected Decision Hub, Solution Hub, and Decision Scenario are live;
2. their actual evidence states are visible and truthful;
3. the pages link to each other and relevant existing decisions;
4. crawlability, canonical identity, and sitemap treatment are recorded;
5. eligible derived assets are generated from the canonical intelligence;
6. selected distribution actions are completed or recorded as failed with retries;
7. measurement begins without blocking publication;
8. missing or changed evidence creates refresh work;
9. material updates can revise and redistribute the live work;
10. the next daily release remains unblocked.

## 21. Governing statement

> Publish one connected decision set every day. Distribute it wherever it can help real people, make it understandable and attributable to legitimate machines, preserve control over access and reuse, learn from what happens, and update the live work whenever better evidence appears.
